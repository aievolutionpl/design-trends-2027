# Seven directions from the video

These are paraphrases and practical interpretations, not a ranking. Timestamps refer to the [video supplied by the user](https://youtu.be/6m7Y5SKXhks). See [sources](sources.md).

## 1. Handmade and analog — 0:32
**Look:** physical matter, tactile edges, photography, paint, thread or cut paper against a calm digital grid. Choose one material meaningful to the brand and one dominant lighting direction; preserve real irregularities.
**Use:** artisan packaging, food posters, editorial covers, campaign hero imagery.
**Implement:** photograph or scan an authorized real artifact, crop with generous space for editable headline, combine with a restrained typeface. In web, use responsive optimized image assets and maintain text as HTML. Generated imitations are not evidence of handmade production.
**Avoid/test:** don't overlay generic grain everywhere or sacrifice contrast. Inspect material detail and headline at final size and confirm image rights.

## 2. Cute-morphism / character mascots — 2:43
**Look:** memorable, simple silhouette, limited colors, a distinctive imperfection and a small set of consistent facial expressions. Define character role, proportions and voice before adding poses.
**Use:** approachable onboarding, education, event identity, friendly brand campaigns where the audience welcomes it.
**Implement:** build a reusable SVG character sheet, including static, small-size and monochrome versions; attach labels and real controls to any product interaction. Provide reduced-motion behavior if animated.
**Avoid/test:** cuteness does not create trust or excuse hidden limitations; never let a mascot disguise risk or interrupt tasks. Test recognition and tone with intended users, including skeptics.

## 3. Process and imperfections — 4:33
**Look:** real sketches, iterations, annotations and before/after arranged as an intelligible narrative; keep one dominant final outcome visible. Choose authentic marks and evidence rather than simulated chaos.
**Use:** case studies, project launches, carousels, creative portfolios.
**Implement:** capture actual milestones and dates with permission, sequence three to five meaningful decisions, caption what changed and why; disclose AI involvement accurately. Use editable type and alt text for meaningful images.
**Avoid/test:** don't fabricate sketches, failures, handcrafted provenance or client approvals; hide sensitive work. Check that a reader can understand the result even without the backstory.

## 4. Found, real textures — 6:11
**Look:** material connected to the subject—packaging, paper, fabric, concrete, food-safe surfaces—photographed or scanned. One authentic texture should dominate; balance it with a clean information zone.
**Use:** album covers, local identities, print posters, product storytelling.
**Implement:** capture authorized material in even light, retain a high-resolution master, clean only distractions, mask and compress for the output. In CSS use a background image with a solid-color fallback, not rasterized text.
**Avoid/test:** don't use irrelevant stock grain as a substitute for a story or place small text over unpredictable detail. Check contrast, tiling, file weight and rights.

## 5. Surrealism — 7:24
**Look:** make one real-world rule strange—scale, gravity, context or material—while keeping perspective, shadows and surroundings coherent. Let the object carry the visual joke.
**Use:** launch campaigns, editorial illustrations, cultural posters, conceptual product ads.
**Implement:** write a one-sentence impossible premise, build a shot with a consistent light source and realistic reference scale; composite authorized photos or render 3D. Keep headline and CTA separate, editable and readable. Identify synthetic or composited scenes where they could mislead.
**Avoid/test:** don't introduce multiple unrelated visual tricks or imply a fictitious public installation was real. Check product shape, perspective, rights and audience comprehension.

## 6. Personality typography — 8:25
**Look:** expressive custom lettering, handwriting or material-built headline; support it with a quiet, legible text face. Decide on a repeatable motif such as irregular baseline or cut-paper notches, not arbitrary distortion.
**Use:** campaigns, covers, independent labels and typographic identities.
**Implement:** draw a short headline as vector lettering or choose a licensed distinctive font; keep longer content live text. Verify required language glyphs, optical spacing, mobile wraps and font file size; preserve accessible text for decorative SVG lettering.
**Avoid/test:** don't mistake novelty for legibility or use a single decorative face for body text. Read at actual sizes and test the copy with the intended audience.

## 7. Deliberately naive / 'dumb design' — 9:33
**Look:** intentional amateurishness: plain paper, clip art, awkward alignment, clashing but limited colors, basic type and one playful oversized starburst. This is the author's explicitly more speculative proposal, not a license to ignore craft.
**Use:** grassroots event flyers, comedic campaigns or subcultures where irreverence fits the brand.
**Implement:** reserve a clear, consistently aligned information block for title, place, date and action; use deliberate irregularity in decorative layers. Keep a straightforward accessible digital version and confirm asset licenses.
**Avoid/test:** don't confuse careless UX, inaccessible text or false amateur provenance with character. At final size, ask a reader to find the event details and action promptly.
