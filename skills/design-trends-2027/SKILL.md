# Design directions: selection and application

Answer in the user's language (Polish or English, including Polish briefs); preserve their terminology. These are creative hypotheses, not measured 2027 trends or a ranking. Directions 1–7 interpret the supplied video; 8–20 are independent editorial additions based on separate sources. Do not present the video's opinions as established facts.

## Pre-flight and selection

Before producing work, check the requested format and dimensions, audience, copy/CTA, brand constraints, asset availability and rights, target language/glyphs, and technical or accessibility constraints. Distinguish supplied material from assumptions; identify blockers and fallback choices. Do not claim any check passed without inspecting the actual output.

## Choose before designing

1. Identify medium, objective, audience, brand tone, essential message/CTA, available licensed assets, constraints and output. If a missing detail blocks a consequential decision, ask; otherwise state a reasonable assumption and proceed.
2. Use the table to shortlist. Choose **one lead and zero to two supporting directions**; explain fit, one trade-off and why the most tempting alternative is less suitable. An explicit user choice wins unless it conflicts with accessibility or another hard constraint; explain and adapt. If none fits, say so rather than force one.
3. Read only the chosen entries in [application recipes](references/application-recipes.md). For implementation and origin of 1–7 consult [video directions](references/video-directions.md); for 8–10 consult [additional directions](references/additional-directions.md); for 11–20 use the recipes and sources. Load [sources and limits](references/sources.md) only when attribution or evidence matters.
4. Copy the selected Polish agent prompt from the recipe, fill its bracketed brief fields, and adapt its instructions to the user’s language and requested artifact; do not treat it as an image-generation prompt by default. Translate the recipe into a deliverable-specific art direction: hierarchy and grid; palette with foreground/background roles; type and glyph coverage; real or synthetic imagery; motion and fallback; production and rights. Preserve existing brand rules. Provide concrete decisions, not just adjectives. For a website implement semantic interactive controls; for a graphic keep text editable and export to the requested dimensions; for a brand define reusable rules and variants. Actually create the requested artifact if tools and inputs permit.
5. Evaluate against the recipe's QA and the shared gate below. State what was tested versus proposed or unavailable. Do not invent performance results, source history, real photographs, approvals or handmade provenance.

| Direction | Choose when | Avoid as lead when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Handmade / analog | tactile craft or human warmth is relevant | physical provenance must be proved but no artifact exists |
| 2 Cute character | friendly guidance fits the audience | serious risk or dense work makes a mascot intrusive |
| 3 Process / imperfection | real decisions tell the story | process is confidential or undocumented |
| 4 Real textures | material is part of brand or product | texture competes with dense information |

| 6 Expressive typography | short message should be the visual | long text is the primary experience |
| 7 Deliberately naive | playful irreverence matches audience | trust-critical tasks require a sober tone |
| 8 Dynamic materials | layers clarify floating controls | dense copy or variable backgrounds undermine contrast |
| 9 Expressive geometry | reusable brand shapes differentiate interface | recognisable conventional controls are paramount |
| 10 Purposeful motion | orientation, feedback or sequence needs motion | static output or motion cannot add meaning |

| 11 Playful retro computing | editorial technology stories or cultural events | real controls must never be confused with decoration |
| 12 Quiet editorial | calm reading and contemplative stories | energetic visual spectacle is the objective |

## Missing resources and shared QA

If no authorized photo, texture, process artifact, brand font, logo or reference is supplied, never claim it exists. Request it when essential; otherwise use a clearly labelled placeholder, licensed alternative or a simple original geometric substitute, document the substitution and leave a replacement point. Verify font license and all needed glyphs (including Polish when applicable). For unsupported effects, supply an opaque/static fallback. Keep core copy, controls and meaning independent of imagery and animation.

Check actual output size, visual hierarchy, CTA, readability and rights. For web check responsive layout, contrast against the actual background, keyboard and focus, semantic labels, reduced motion and performance. Test screen-reader behavior where applicable. Contrast or accessibility compliance must not be asserted without the relevant tests. If comparison is requested, define a hypothesis, neutral alternative and task measure, without inventing results.

## Verification and reporting

After production, inspect the actual deliverable against the selected recipe and shared QA gate. Report each relevant check as passed, failed, or not tested, with the evidence or reason. Fix defects within scope; name any missing assets, unverified rights, browser/print limitations and remaining blockers. Never substitute a proposed test for a completed one.
