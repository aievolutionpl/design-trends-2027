# Design Trends 2027

Use these directions as creative hypotheses, not a popularity ranking or a promise about 2027. The first seven are adapted from the supplied video transcript; three further visual directions are editorial additions grounded in separate sources. The video author's view that minimalism is dying is an opinion, not a fact. The directions overlap and can be combined carefully.

## Choose and produce

1. Identify medium, audience, brand, message, assets, constraints and deliverable. If assets are absent, say what is needed; do not invent photographs, source history or performance results.
2. Choose **one lead direction** and at most two supporting ones. Explain the fit and trade-off. For style details read [video directions](references/video-directions.md) for handmade, mascots, process, found texture, surrealism, expressive type and naive design; read [additional directions](references/additional-directions.md) for glass, expressive geometry and purposeful motion. Read [sources and limitations](references/sources.md) for provenance and evidence.
3. Specify hierarchy, palette, typography, composition, imagery/material, interaction if relevant, and production constraints. Give actionable steps appropriate to the requested medium and an anti-pattern to avoid. Create the requested asset or code when tools and inputs permit, rather than stopping at a moodboard.
4. Check message and CTA first, then readability at actual output size, brand fit, copyright and truthful process claims. For interactive work check keyboard, focus, contrast, screen readers where applicable, reduced motion and performance; test in context rather than claiming compliance from a token or automated check.
5. When evaluation is requested, propose a testable hypothesis, a neutral alternative and a task-based measure. Do not invent metrics or assert adoption, effectiveness or authenticity. Explain untested assumptions.

## Quick examples

- A coffee poster: lead with handmade collage; use a real bean-bag scan as a supporting texture, set a clear offer and test it at phone size. Do not imply a generated image was physically crafted.
- A B2B dashboard: use expressive geometry sparingly in selected navigation; retain conventional controls and clear focus states. Skip mascot and surrealism if they harm the task.
- A culture event flyer: use deliberate naive design with one clip-art accent and a clean date/location block; check the information remains immediately legible.

If the brief lacks a suitable real asset, use a clearly labelled placeholder or request one. If a font lacks required characters or licensing, use an appropriately licensed alternative; do not present fallback text as a finished brand treatment. If a visual effect lacks browser support, supply a usable simpler fallback.
